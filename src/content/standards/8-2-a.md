## Committee Findings
The Committee reviewed the institution’s narrative and supporting documentation describing its Academic Program Review (APR) process, which requires programs to identify student learning outcomes, assess achievement, and develop improvement plans as needed. The institution submitted a sample of seven APR reports from the 2022-2023 and 2023-2024 academic years, along with two general education assessment reports and excerpts from Tennessee Board of Regents academic audits. The institution did not discuss how the Academic Program Review reports submitted were selected or how they represent the institution’s educational Programs.

Additionally, the Committee reviewed the institution’s three year APR schedule by cohort. The sample included reports from Cohorts A and C. However, the Committee could not locate APR reports for programs in Cohort B or for the University Parallel AA/AS programs, which represent the largest number of completers according to the Institutional Summary Form.

Across the APR reports submitted, the Committee found information related to student learning outcomes, assessment methods, results, and planned actions. The Tennessee Board of Regents academic audits and general education assessments similarly included outcomes, assessment locations, and sample data. However, the Committee could not locate documentation demonstrating the use of assessment results to seek improvement across the supporting documentation reviewed. The Committee also could not determine whether the sample provided represents the institution’s educational programs.

The Committee could not locate a rationale indicating that the programs in the sample were representative of all educational programs offered. Therefore, the Committee could not determine that the institution assesses student learning outcomes for all educational programs and provides evidence of seeking improvement based on analysis of those results.

## Response Narrative
Volunteer State Community College appreciates the Committee's careful review of the institution's Fifth-Year Interim Report and the opportunity to clarify the evidence supporting compliance with Standard 8.2.a. The Committee's comments centered on two primary areas: (1) whether the submitted sample represented the institution's full portfolio of educational programs and (2) whether the documentation clearly demonstrated how assessment results were used to inform improvements in student learning.

While the institution's original submission included evidence of student learning assessment and examples of resulting improvements, it did not sufficiently explain the methodology used to select the sample of Academic Program Reviews (APRs), nor did it consistently make explicit the connection between assessment findings and the improvement initiatives that resulted from those findings. This response addresses both concerns by providing comprehensive documentation for all educational programs and additional evidence illustrating how assessment results systematically inform continuous improvement.

### Comprehensive Representation of Educational Programs
Volunteer State Community College assesses student learning outcomes for every educational program through a structured Academic Program Review (APR) process. Academic programs participate in a comprehensive three-year review cycle organized into Cohorts A, B, and C. University Parallel (AA/AS) programs follow a separate review schedule aligned with the same institutional assessment expectations. Together, these review cycles ensure every educational program undergoes regular evaluation of student learning outcomes.[cite](/documents/8-2-a/10_APR_Cohort_A.pdf "APR Cohort A Results") [cite](/documents/8-2-a/20_APR_Cohort_B.pdf "APR Cohort B Results") [cite](/documents/8-2-a/30_APR_Cohort_C.pdf "APR Cohort C Results") [cite](/documents/8-2-a/40_APR_University_Parallel.pdf "APR University Parallel Results")

The original Fifth-Year Interim Report included APRs from Cohorts A and C during the 2022-2023 and 2023-2024 academic years. While these reports accurately reflected the assessment process, the institution recognizes the submission did not sufficiently explain the rationale for selecting those reports or demonstrate how the sample represented all educational programs.

To address this concern, Volunteer State Community College is submitting the complete Academic Program Reviews for all programs in Cohorts A, B, and C, along with University Parallel (AA/AS) programs, representing the institution's full three-year review cycle.[cite](/documents/8-2-a/50_Assessment_Timeline.pdf "Assessment Timeline") This expanded documentation demonstrates student learning assessment occurs across the institution's entire portfolio of educational programs, including programs with the largest student enrollments. The requested Cohort B assessment begins on page 203.[cite](/documents/8-2-a/50_Assessment_Timeline.pdf "Assessment Timeline")

### Using Assessment Results to Inform Improvement
The Committee also noted the original submission did not clearly demonstrate how assessment findings informed program improvements. The institution appreciates this observation. Although the APRs documented assessment results and identified improvement initiatives, the relationship between the assessment findings and resulting actions was not always made explicit within the narrative.

Faculty review student learning outcome assessment results annually as part of the Academic Program Review process. Assessment findings are analyzed to identify strengths, performance gaps, and opportunities for improvement. Based on this analysis, faculty develop targeted initiatives designed to improve student learning, curriculum, instructional practices, or student support services. These initiatives are documented within the APR process, entered into the institution's assessment management system, monitored through annual progress updates, and evaluated during subsequent assessment cycles to determine their effectiveness.

To make these connections more explicit, the institution has developed an Initiative Alignment Crosswalks for Cohorts A,[cite](/documents/8-2-a/60_Cohort_A_Crosswalk.pdf "Cohort A Crosswalk") B,[cite](/documents/8-2-a/70_Cohort_B_Crosswalk.pdf "Cohort B Crosswalk") C,[cite](/documents/8-2-a/80_Cohort_C_Crosswalk.pdf "Cohort C Crosswalk") and University Parallel.[cite](/documents/8-2-a/90_UTP_Crosswalk.pdf "University Parallel Crosswalk") This document illustrates the direct relationship between:

- Assessment findings identified through Academic Program Review;
- Improvement initiatives developed in response to those findings;
- Program outcomes resulting from implementation of those initiatives.

Rather than simply documenting assessment activities and subsequent changes, the crosswalk demonstrates how faculty used assessment evidence to inform decisions designed to improve student learning. Examples included within the crosswalk show assessment findings leading to curriculum revisions, instructional enhancements, student support initiatives, and other targeted improvements. Where reassessment has occurred, the crosswalk also documents evidence of resulting improvements in student learning or program performance. For initiatives currently underway, the document identifies ongoing monitoring and future assessment activities.

Collectively, the APR documentation and Initiative Alignment Crosswalk demonstrate an institution-wide, systematic process in which assessment results are analyzed, discussed by faculty, translated into improvement initiatives, implemented, and reassessed as part of the College's continuous improvement cycle.

### Conclusion
With the expanded documentation submitted in response to the Committee's comments, Volunteer State Community College provides clear evidence that:

- Student learning outcomes are assessed for every educational program.
- Academic Program Reviews represent the institution's complete portfolio of educational programs.
- Faculty analyze assessment findings to identify opportunities for improvement.
- Assessment results directly inform curricular, pedagogical, and programmatic improvements.
- Improvement initiatives are documented, monitored, and evaluated through an ongoing cycle of continuous improvement.

The institution respectfully submits this additional documentation as evidence of full compliance with SACSCOC Standard 8.2.a.
